Development and assessment in a complete season forecast

Does the margin a model wins by on the seasons used to select it survive on later seasons?

A complete forecast has three jobs: fitting candidates, selecting a recipe, and assessing the frozen result. Here the candidates are fitted and compared on seasons 5 and 6, and the chosen recipe is frozen before seasons 7 and 8 are scored.

On seasons 7 and 8 the frozen model scores 0.2376 against 0.2500: 0.2500 - 0.2376 = 0.0124, a smaller margin than on development. Report it; do not select a new recipe from this score.

Worked steps

  1. Season 7: 0.2500 - 0.2349 = 0.0151.
  2. Season 8: 0.2500 - 0.2404 = 0.0096.
  3. Both seasons: 0.2500 - 0.2376 = 0.0124.

Use the idea

Keep a frozen artifact and score it once on later data. If you change the recipe after seeing that score, the later seasons become development data too.

Where the conclusion applies

528 synthetic games, 12 teams and eight seasons with a fixed seed. One fixture and two related seasons do not establish statistical significance or a historical gain.

Check your understanding: Why is the assessment margin usually smaller than the development margin?
The model was chosen because it scored well on development, so part of that margin is selection luck.
