Demonstration 1 of 1

Wilson intervals on a fixed teaching table of mention counts

Using a fixed teaching table of mention counts, what can an observed mention rate support, and how uncertain is it in a small selected sample of write-ups?

The activity keeps a fixed teaching table of 161 write-ups and four mention counts to show how a Wilson interval works; the revised chapter does not report these counts. Wilson intervals show how much a rate from 161 write-ups could move, and a minimum fraction turns the counts into a shortlist of things to check, not advice on where to spend effort.

In the activity's fixed teaching table, 107 / 161 = 0.665 of the write-ups mention feature engineering, with a Wilson interval of 0.589 to 0.733. A minimum of 0.40 shortlists 3 of 4 techniques: Feature engineering, Ensembling, Post-processing. These are teaching counts for the Wilson interval, not the book's current corpus figures, and the list is for verification only: a mention is not an ablation and says nothing about cause.

Worked steps

  1. Teaching table: 161 write-ups and four mention counts, not current corpus figures; none is ablation-checked.
  2. Feature engineering: 107 / 161 = 0.665, interval 0.589 to 0.733, shortlisted.
  3. Ensembling: 92 / 161 = 0.571, interval 0.494 to 0.645, shortlisted.
  4. Post-processing: 73 / 161 = 0.453, interval 0.378 to 0.531, shortlisted.
  5. Pseudo-labeling: 13 / 161 = 0.081, interval 0.048 to 0.133, below the minimum.

Use the idea

Keep a source-linked ledger of explicit mentions, verified use and ablation evidence as separate columns. Before borrowing a technique, compare it with your unchanged baseline on your own task and keep an independent audit.

Where the conclusion applies

The 161 write-ups and four counts are the activity's fixed teaching table, transcribed from an earlier draft and not repeated in the revised chapter text. They are not the book's current corpus figures and were never independently verified. A Wilson interval describes a binomial sampling model, which may not fit a selected set of winning write-ups. Absence of a mention is not absence of use, and a high rate does not show a technique caused winning.
