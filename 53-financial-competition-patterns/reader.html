This is a constructed teaching example. Its results describe these supplied inputs, not a measured competition gain.

Assumptions

['Synthetic single-date cross-section; both targets are known only for demonstration.', 'Residual component is centered and orthogonal to the factor.', 'The competition metric determines which target is relevant.']

What the result means

Neutralization reduces factor exposure and helps the neutral target in this construction. It can worsen the factor-retaining target. Shared exposure is not automatically leakage; correctness depends on the available information and scored target.

Apply this to your model

Start with the official financial target and scorer, not a general trading interpretation. Audit feature timestamps and target latency, then compute market regime summaries once per date before applying temporal windows. Fit neutralization only against exposures available at prediction time, and compare raw versus neutralized features on deployment-matched folds. For future cross-sectional targets, retain a forward audit even if grouped-year interpolation helps tuning. Evaluate target transforms and clipping under the actual metric. Reject changes justified solely by historical correlation or by an assumed Sharpe/factor-neutralized score that the competition does not use.
