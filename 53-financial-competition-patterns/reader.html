What partial neutralization removes

What does partial factor neutralization remove, and when does it remove predictive signal?

Neutralization subtracts part of the prediction's projection onto a factor. That lowers factor exposure and moves the prediction toward a target built from the factor-free component. If the scored target keeps the factor effect, the same step moves the prediction away from it.

x_i is the factor exposure of row i, p_i the raw prediction, f the fraction of the fitted factor projection that is removed, b-hat the no-intercept least-squares slope of p on x (2 in this construction), q_i the neutralized prediction, and y the target the error is measured against: either the factor-neutral target (the orthogonal residual part of p) or the factor-retaining target (the raw prediction p itself).

Removing 0.5 of the factor projection leaves an exposure slope of 2 x (1 - 0.5) = 1.00. Errors are 4.67 against the factor-neutral target and 4.67 against the factor-retaining target. The two errors tie, so the prediction fits neither target better. Which one counts depends on the metric the competition scores.

Worked steps

  1. Fraction removed: f = 0.5.
  2. Exposure slope left: 2 x (1 - 0.5) = 1.00.
  3. MSE against the factor-neutral target: 4.67.
  4. MSE against the factor-retaining target: 4.67.
  5. The two errors tie, so the prediction fits neither target better.

Use the idea

Identify the official target and scorer before choosing any neutralization. Fit it only on exposures available at prediction time and compare raw and neutralized versions on folds that match deployment.

Where the conclusion applies

One synthetic cross-section of six rows, with a factor-driven part and an orthogonal residual, and both targets known only for demonstration. Shared exposure is not automatically leakage. The result describes this construction, not any competition metric.

Check your understanding: At f = 0.8, which target does the neutralized prediction fit better, and what are the two errors?
The factor-neutral target: its error is 0.75 and the factor-retaining error is 11.95. The remaining slope is 2 x (1 - 0.8) = 0.40.
