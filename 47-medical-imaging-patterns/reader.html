1. What a CT window width keeps and clips

Demonstration 1 of 1

What a CT window width keeps and clips

What tissue contrast is retained or clipped by a chosen CT window width?

The activity keeps the window center at 40 HU and applies a chosen width to seven constructed values. Values beyond the edges are clipped to 0 or 255 and the rest are rescaled linearly. A narrow window spends its 255 levels on a small interval; a wide one keeps more values distinct but gives less contrast near the center.

A window 80 HU wide spreads 255 levels over 80 HU: 255 / 80 = 3.1875 levels per HU. 4 of the 7 values fall outside 0 to 80 HU and are clipped to 0 or 255, leaving 3 distinct intensities: 7 - 3 = 4 fewer than the number of values.

Worked steps

  1. Center 40 HU and width 80 HU give a window from 0 to 80 HU.
  2. Levels per HU inside the window: 255 / 80 = 3.1875.
  3. Values outside the window: 4, set to the nearest edge.
  4. The center value 40 HU always maps to 127.5.
  5. Distinct intensities among 7 values: 3.

Use the idea

Keep the raw HU values and build explicit window channels for the task, checking representative conversions against the DICOM metadata. Compare window choices with an unchanged backbone and patient-disjoint folds.

Where the conclusion applies

The seven values are illustrative HU numbers, not a patient scan, and the center is fixed at 40 HU. The linear clipping rule is simplified. The page does not say which window is clinically appropriate.

Check your understanding: At a width of 400 HU, how many values are clipped, and how does the contrast per HU compare with 80 HU?
Two values (-1000 and 700 HU) are clipped and all seven intensities are distinct. Contrast falls from 255 / 80 = 3.1875 levels per HU to 255 / 400 = 0.6375.
