Chapter 13

Hyperparameter Tuning with Optuna

Separate the search best from an independent assessment.

A search winner carries the benefit of selection, so keep an assessment outside the loop. One demonstration compares the best search score with an untouched audit score as the number of trials grows.

Every example is a constructed teaching example. Its results describe the declared inputs of the chapter activity, not a measured competition gain.

1. Best search score versus independent audit score

Demonstration 1 of 1

Best search score versus independent audit score

When a hyperparameter search examines more trials, does the audit score of the chosen trial improve as the search score does?

A search selects the candidate that looks best on the data it keeps examining, so the winning score contains the benefit of selection. The audit scores here are revealed after the choice and never used to make it, so they show how much of the search score was selection.

n is the number of trials examined, s_t the search score of trial t, a_t its audit score, t* the chosen trial and Delta the amount by which the chosen search score exceeds its audit score.

After 4 trials the search picks trial 4 with search score 0.887; its untouched audit score is 0.878, so the search score overstates it by 0.887 - 0.878 = 0.009.

Worked steps

  1. Trials examined: 4.
  2. Highest search score among them: trial 4 at 0.887.
  3. Audit score of that trial, never used to choose it: 0.878.
  4. Gap: 0.887 - 0.878 = 0.009.

Use the idea

Define the search space, metric direction and budget first, and keep an assessment outside the adaptive loop. Apply the frozen recipe to it once instead of extending the search until the audit looks better.

Where the conclusion applies

Eight fixed hypothetical candidates with one search score and one audit score each. One fixed sequence shows the direction of selection optimism, not its typical size, and it is not a Kaggle result.

Check your understanding: With 2 trials examined, which trial is chosen and how large is the gap between its search and audit scores?
Trial 2, with search score 0.884 and audit score 0.880, so the gap is 0.884 - 0.880 = 0.004. Trial 3 does not beat it on search (0.882), so 3 trials give the same choice.
