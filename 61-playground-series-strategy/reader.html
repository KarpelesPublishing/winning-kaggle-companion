What the result means

A budget can favor several short diagnostics over one long refinement, but the choice follows the declared values. Change those assumptions for your project rather than adopting 50 experiments as a universal target. Feasible alternatives expose the tradeoff for a human decision.

Apply this to your model

Audit folds, metric direction, source-data rules, and duplicate overlap before scheduling experiments. Estimate each experiment’s runtime and the concrete uncertainty it resolves. Reserve time for validation, then choose independent representation, feature, external-data, or ensemble checks rather than repeatedly polishing the same model. Use identical folds for comparisons and a noise tolerance when reading CV/LB changes. Preserve existing source labels and use sign-safe ratios for signed features. Reject experiments that consume the remaining budget without resolving a decision, and freeze a reproducible final submission early enough to verify execution.
