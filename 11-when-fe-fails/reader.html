Chapter 11

When Feature Engineering Fails

Separate variance from label information.

A failed feature comparison is useful when it names a narrower next test. One demonstration shows an axis that holds nearly all the variance while a small axis carries the labels.

1. Variance kept versus label information kept

Demonstration 1 of 1

Variance kept versus label information kept

If a reduction keeps nearly all the variance, has it kept the information that separates the labels?

Variance measures how far rows move along an axis. Prediction depends on whether that movement tracks the label. Here the large axis moves by plus or minus ten with no relation to the label, and the small axis carries the label at every scale you can choose.

At signal scale 0.1 the nuisance axis holds 100 / (100 + 0.01) = 0.9999 of the variance, yet its sign rule is right on 0.50 of rows. The signal axis holds almost none of the variance and is right on 1.00.

Worked steps

  1. Nuisance variance: 100.
  2. Signal variance at scale 0.1: 0.01.
  3. Nuisance share: 100 / (100 + 0.01) = 0.9999.
  4. Sign-rule accuracy: nuisance 0.50, signal 1.00.

Use the idea

Before trusting an explained-variance cutoff, compare raw and reduced inputs on the same held-out rows with the competition metric, fitting any learned reduction inside each fold.

Where the conclusion applies

Four rows, two centered orthogonal axes, no eigensolver and no fitted model. This does not show that PCA always fails or that any other reduction succeeds, and it does not explain a historical failure.
