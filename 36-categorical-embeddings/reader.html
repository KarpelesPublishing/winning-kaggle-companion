A held-out label inside a smoothed encoding

Does a held-out target influence an encoding through its global prior?

A category mean can be fold-local while the prior it shrinks toward still uses every label. Here the category statistic uses training rows only, yet an all-row prior pulls the encoding toward the validation labels. Stronger smoothing gives that prior more weight, so the dependency grows.

e is the smoothed encoding of the category, m the smoothing count, and pi the prior it falls back toward. The 1 in the numerator is the category's one positive training label and the 1 in the denominator is its one training observation. The training-only prior is 0.5; the all-row prior, which also counts the validation labels, is 0.75.

With smoothing count 10, the all-row encoding is (1 + 10 x 0.75) / (1 + 10) = 8.5 / 11 = 0.7727, and the training-only encoding is 6 / 11 = 0.5455. The gap is 10 x 0.25 / (1 + 10) = 2.5 / 11 = 0.2273. That gap exists only because the all-row prior used the two validation labels.

Worked steps

  1. Training labels [0, 1] give a prior of 0.50; adding the validation labels [1, 1] gives 0.75.
  2. The category has one training observation, with label 1.
  3. Training-only: (1 + 10 x 0.50) / (1 + 10) = 6 / 11 = 0.5455.
  4. All-row prior: (1 + 10 x 0.75) / (1 + 10) = 8.5 / 11 = 0.7727.
  5. Gap, the difference of the two: 10 x 0.25 / (1 + 10) = 2.5 / 11 = 0.2273.

Use the idea

Trace every target-derived quantity in the encoder, including the prior and the fallback for unseen categories, and compute each one from the training rows of the current partition only.

Where the conclusion applies

Four binary labels: two training rows [0, 1] and two validation rows [1, 1], with one training observation of the category and a label of one. The gap shows a dependency on held-out labels, not the size of any score change.

Check your understanding: At smoothing count 3, what are the two encodings and their gap?
The all-row encoding is (1 + 3 x 0.75) / 4 = 3.25 / 4 = 0.8125 and the training-only encoding is (1 + 3 x 0.5) / 4 = 2.5 / 4 = 0.625, so the gap is 0.1875.
