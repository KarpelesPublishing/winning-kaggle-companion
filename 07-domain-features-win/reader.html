Domain Features as Testable Hypotheses

Compare a current amount to the entity's past history.

A domain feature is a hypothesis that needs an availability check and a held-out comparison. One demonstration contrasts a current amount with past-only history and with a baseline that leaks the current row.

1. A current amount against past entity history

Demonstration 1 of 1

A current amount against past entity history

How does a purchase compare with what the same entity did before, and what changes if the current purchase leaks into its own baseline?

An amount gains meaning when it is compared with the entity's prior behavior. The four earlier purchases stay fixed at a mean of 25, so the ratio and the deviation both grow with the current amount. A baseline that already contains the current purchase moves toward it and weakens the very contrast you want.

x is the current amount, h-bar is the mean of the four prior purchases by the same entity, rho is the amount-to-history ratio, d is the past-only deviation and d_self the deviation from a mean that already includes x (the mean of the four prior purchases and x together, (4 h-bar + x) / 5).

Predict first. A customer's four prior purchases average 25, and the current purchase is 120. The past-only deviation is 95. Is the self-included deviation larger, smaller or the same?

Against the past-only mean the deviation is 120.0 - 25.0 = 95.0 and the ratio is 120.0 / 25.0 = 4.80. Once the current purchase is inside its own baseline the mean moves to 44.0, so the deviation shrinks to 120.0 - 44.0 = 76.0. An amount contrast is not a fraud probability.

Worked steps

  1. Mean of the four prior purchases: 25.0.
  2. Past-only deviation: 120.0 - 25.0 = 95.0.
  3. Past-only ratio: 120.0 / 25.0 = 4.80.
  4. Baseline that includes the current purchase: (100.0 + 120.0) / 5 = 44.0.
  5. Self-included deviation: 120.0 - 44.0 = 76.0.

Use the idea

Sort records by prediction time and compute prior-only histories with a documented policy for simultaneous events. Add support counts and fallbacks for sparse or unseen entities, and compare absolute values, ratios and deviations on identical held-out units.

Where the conclusion applies

The entity key is reliable (a pseudo-identity from anonymized fields is only a hypothesis), only prior history is used, and the current amount is known at prediction time. Four prior purchases is sparse. A high ratio neither labels fraud nor promises a ranking gain.

Check your understanding: At a current amount of 500, what are the past-only and self-included deviations?
Past-only: 500 - 25 = 475. The self-included baseline is (100 + 500) / 5 = 120, so the deviation is 500 - 120 = 380, which is four fifths of 475.
