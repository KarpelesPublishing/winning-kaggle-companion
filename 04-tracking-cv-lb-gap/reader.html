Chapter 4

Tracking the CV-LB Gap

Identical gaps can come from different mechanisms.

A CV-LB gap is a discrepancy to investigate, not a diagnosis. One demonstration gives a blend and an unchanged model the same gap and shows that only the paired local comparison separates them.

Identical gaps, different mechanisms

If a blend and an unchanged model show exactly the same CV-LB gap, what does the gap tell you about the blend?

A gap is a difference between two scores, not a diagnosis. Here both scenarios receive the same public adjustment, so the gap is identical, yet only one has lower paired local loss than the baseline. Read the local-improvement panel before deciding which scenario supports a blending benefit.

Blend: LB 0.018 - CV 0.020 = -0.002. Unchanged: LB 0.023 - CV 0.025 = -0.002. The gaps match, but the paired local improvement is 0.025 - 0.020 = 0.005 for the blend and 0.000 for the unchanged scenario. The gap alone cannot tell them apart.

Worked steps

  1. Public adjustment applied to both scenarios: 0.002.
  2. Blend gap: 0.018 - 0.020 = -0.002.
  3. Unchanged gap: 0.023 - 0.025 = -0.002.
  4. Blend local improvement: 0.025 - 0.020 = 0.005.
  5. Unchanged local improvement: 0.000, because its errors equal the baseline errors.

Use the idea

Keep predictions on identical validation rows for the champion and each challenger, and compare errors by the meaningful unit such as customer or season. If the signals disagree, record the competing explanations and the check that could separate them.

Where the conclusion applies

The local errors are constructed and the public adjustment is the same for both scenarios by design. The gap sign cannot identify a causal mechanism, and a stable gap does not certify every feature.

Check your understanding: At a public adjustment of 0.010, what are the two public losses and the two gaps?
Blend: 0.020 - 0.010 = 0.010 public loss. Unchanged: 0.025 - 0.010 = 0.015. Both gaps are -0.010, so the gap alone still cannot separate them.
