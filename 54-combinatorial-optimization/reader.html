How does annealing temperature affect exploration while preserving the best feasible tour?

This is a constructed teaching example. Its results describe these supplied inputs, not a measured competition gain.

Assumptions

['Constructed Euclidean closed tour; every reversal remains feasible.', 'Fixed temperature is a mechanism experiment, not a full cooling schedule.', 'Temperature has the same units as tour-cost changes.']

What the result means

Higher temperature can accept more worsening moves, but one finite seeded run need not improve monotonically with temperature. Best-so-far retention prevents returning a worse answer than the initial tour even when the current tour deteriorates.

Apply this to your model

Inspect the official solution format, legal moves, hard constraints, and objective units. Build a scorer and feasibility validator before selecting a search algorithm. Construct a deterministic feasible baseline, then profile neighbor generation and cost evaluation. Add annealing with a temperature calibrated from the actual distribution of worsening deltas, retaining the best feasible solution separately from the current chain. For scheduling, include every precedence relation explicitly. Compare multiple seeds under the same wall-clock budget. Reject a search improvement if it violates constraints, omits return-path validation, or wins only because it consumed more compute.
