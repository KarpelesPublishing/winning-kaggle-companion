1. Temperature, exploration and the retained best tour

Demonstration 1 of 1

Temperature, exploration and the retained best tour

How does annealing temperature change exploration while the best feasible tour is preserved?

Annealing accepts a tour that is longer with probability exp(-delta/T), so a higher temperature explores more. The search also stores the best tour separately from the current one, which is why a chain that drifts upward never makes the reported answer worse than what it has already seen.

T is the fixed temperature, in the same units as tour length. Delta is the cost change of one candidate move (reverse a segment of the tour), c is closed-tour length, and P(accept) is the chance the move is taken.

The search improved the starting tour by 16.55 - 13.30 = 3.25 while accepting 3 worsening moves of 22 accepted. The chain ended 14.49 - 13.30 = 1.19 above the best tour, and only best-so-far retention keeps the reported answer at the lower cost.

Worked steps

  1. Temperature T = 1.00; worsening moves are accepted with probability e^(-delta/T).
  2. Starting tour length: 16.55.
  3. Moves accepted: 22 of 40, of which 3 made the tour longer.
  4. Best tour kept: 13.30, improvement 16.55 - 13.30 = 3.25.
  5. Current tour at the end: 14.49, which is 14.49 - 13.30 = 1.19 above the best.

Use the idea

Keep the best feasible solution apart from the current chain, and calibrate the temperature from the actual distribution of worsening deltas. Compare several seeds under the same time budget before crediting a setting.

Where the conclusion applies

One closed tour through six constructed points, a fixed seed, and a constant temperature rather than a cooling schedule. Every segment reversal stays feasible. One finite path need not improve steadily with temperature, and repeated seeds are not shown.

Check your understanding: At T = 0.01 the current tour length ends equal to the best length. Why?
No worsening moves were accepted (0 of 18 accepted moves), so the current length never rose and ended at 13.30, the lowest length the chain reached, which is the best kept.
