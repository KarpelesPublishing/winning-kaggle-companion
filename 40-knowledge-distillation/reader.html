Chapter 40

Knowledge Distillation

Soften teacher targets without leaking evaluation labels.

Use eligible teacher targets to test a student's quality and cost, and judge it on held-out labels. One demonstration shows how temperature softens a teacher probability and what order and entropy remain.

Every example is a constructed teaching example. Its results describe the declared inputs of the chapter activity, not a measured competition gain.

1. What a softened teacher target keeps

Demonstration 1 of 1

What a softened teacher target keeps

What information remains when a teacher probability is softened?

Softening divides the teacher's log odds by T and maps back through the logistic function, so targets move toward one half while keeping their order. Entropy rises because the targets are less extreme. A student fit to these targets receives only what the teacher already knew, softened.

p_i is a teacher probability (0.1, 0.5 or 0.9), T the distillation temperature, sigma the logistic function 1 / (1 + e^(-u)), q_i the softened target, and H-bar the mean binary entropy of the three softened targets, in nats.

For a teacher probability of 0.9 the log odds are ln(9) = 2.1972. Dividing by T = 2.0 gives 2.1972 / 2.0 = 1.0986, and the logistic function turns that into 0.7500. The target for 0.5 stays at 0.5 and the order of the three targets never changes.

Worked steps

  1. Teacher probabilities: 0.1, 0.5, 0.9.
  2. Log odds of 0.9: ln(9) = 2.1972; of 0.1 the same with a minus sign; of 0.5 zero.
  3. Divide by T = 2.0: 2.1972 / 2.0 = 1.0986.
  4. Logistic function: softened targets 0.2500, 0.5000, 0.7500.
  5. Mean binary entropy of those three targets: 0.6059 nats.

Use the idea

Treat temperature and the hard-label weight as development-selected settings, and check that every teacher target was produced without the labels of the student's evaluation fold.

Where the conclusion applies

Three fixed teacher probabilities, with temperature applied in logit space. No student is fitted. Higher entropy does not show better calibration or a more accurate student.

Check your understanding: At T = 2, what does a teacher probability of 0.9 become, and why is it exactly that value?
0.75. The log odds ln(9) = 2.1972 halve to 1.0986 = ln(3), and 1 / (1 + 1/3) = 0.75, because dividing the log odds by 2 takes the square root of the odds, 9 to 3.
