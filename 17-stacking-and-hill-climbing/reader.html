Stacking and Hill-Climbing

Shrinking one meta coefficient with ridge

How does the ridge penalty change a fitted meta coefficient, and can the training error tell you which penalty to use?

Stacking learns a combination of model predictions, and a ridge penalty shrinks that combination toward zero so it follows the meta-training data less tightly. Larger alpha means a smaller coefficient and a worse fit to the rows it was fitted on.

x_i is the centered meta feature of row i, y_i its target, alpha the ridge penalty, beta hat the fitted coefficient and MSE the training mean squared error over the four rows.

The coefficient is 0.44 / (0.40 + 1) = 0.3143, against 1.10 at alpha 0. Its training MSE is 0.0707, measured on the same four rows used to fit it, so it cannot choose alpha.

Worked steps

  1. Sum of x times y over the four rows: 0.44. Sum of x squared: 0.40.
  2. Coefficient: 0.44 / (0.40 + 1) = 0.3143.
  3. Predictions are the coefficient times x: -0.1257, -0.0629, 0.0629, 0.1257.
  4. Training MSE: 0.0707. Penalty term alpha times coefficient squared: 0.0988.

Use the idea

Collect first-level predictions without each row's label feeding its own prediction, tune the penalty inside the selection boundary, and compare the complete stack with a simple blend on untouched data.

Where the conclusion applies

One centered meta feature, four rows, squared loss and an L2 penalty. The displayed errors are training errors on the fitting rows, so they are not an independent estimate of a stacked model and do not say which alpha generalizes best.

Check your understanding: Why is alpha 0 always the lowest-training-error setting here, and why can't you pick alpha that way?
Alpha 0 is the least-squares fit, with training MSE 0.009 and no shrinkage, so any penalty raises the training error. Alpha has to be chosen on separate held-out data.
