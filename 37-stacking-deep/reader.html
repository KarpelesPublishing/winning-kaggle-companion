Stacking Deep

Look for complementary error before adding a level.

Separate fitting a meta model from assessing the whole stack, and blend before adding a level. One demonstration shows how residual covariance sets the error of a two-model blend.

1. Residual covariance and the error of a blend

Demonstration 1 of 1

Residual covariance and the error of a blend

How does residual covariance explain the error of a two-model blend?

An ensemble gains from complementary error, not from prediction correlation. Both models here track the target closely, yet their mistakes alternate in sign, so the residual covariance is negative and the blend's error falls far below either parent's. Change the weight to see how the gain depends on how errors line up.

Scroll sideways for the whole equation →

w is the weight on model B, MSE_a and MSE_b the mean squared errors of the two models alone, and c_ab the mean of the product of their row residuals (prediction - target). The activity reports the residual covariance, which centers each residual first: it equals c_ab minus the product of the two mean residuals.

Predict first. Each model alone has a mean squared error of 0.1. What is the blend's error at equal weights?

At w = 0.50: 0.2500 x 0.1000 + 0.2500 x 0.1000 + 0.5000 x (-0.0800) = 0.0100. The residual covariance is -0.0900, so the two models' deviations around their average errors move in opposite directions and partly cancel in the blend.

Worked steps

  1. Residuals (prediction - target): model A [0.4, -0.2, 0.4, -0.2], model B [-0.2, 0.4, -0.2, 0.4].
  2. Mean residuals are 0.10 and 0.10; the residual covariance is -0.0900.
  3. Mean residual product c_ab = -0.0800: the covariance plus the product of the mean residuals, (-0.0900) + 0.10 x 0.10 = (-0.0800).
  4. Blend MSE: 0.2500 x 0.1000 + 0.2500 x 0.1000 + 0.5000 x (-0.0800) = 0.0100.

Use the idea

Before adding a model or a stack level, compare residual covariance and paired held-out gains against a simple average, rather than applying a fixed prediction-correlation cutoff.

Where the conclusion applies

Four fixed regression targets and two prediction vectors, with no meta learner trained. These are constructed comparisons: the blend gain here does not show a gain on real data, which needs independent validation.

Check your understanding: With weight 0.25 on model B, what is the blend's mean squared error, and why does weight 0.75 give the same value?
0.75^2 x 0.1 + 0.25^2 x 0.1 + 2 x 0.25 x 0.75 x (-0.08) = 0.05625 + 0.00625 - 0.03 = 0.0325. Both models have the same MSE, so swapping the weights 0.25 and 0.75 leaves every term unchanged.
