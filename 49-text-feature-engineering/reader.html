Chapter 49

Text Feature Engineering

Measure vocabulary coverage before choosing a text representation.

Check term overlap first, then compare word, character and embedding representations under identical splits. One demonstration shows character n-grams keeping coverage where a word vocabulary misses a typo.

1. Character n-grams against a word vocabulary

Demonstration 1 of 1

Character n-grams against a word vocabulary

How can character n-grams preserve coverage when the word vocabulary misses a typo?

Three training product phrases define a word vocabulary and character n-gram vocabularies. Three test phrases contain spelling variants. A typo makes a whole word unknown, but most of its character fragments still appear in training, so short n-grams keep coverage high.

For 'red runing shoe' at width 3, 12 of its 13 character n-grams are in the training vocabulary: 12 / 13 = 0.9231, against word coverage 0.6667 where the misspelled word counts as missing. Widening the n-gram lowers coverage in this example, because more of the fragments span the altered letters and stop matching.

Worked steps

  1. Character n-gram width: 3; the training phrases give 36 distinct n-grams.
  2. 'red runing shoe' has 13 n-grams, 12 of them found in the vocabulary.
  3. Occurrence coverage: 12 / 13 = 0.9231.
  4. Word coverage of the same phrase: 2 / 3 words known = 0.6667.
  5. Occurrence coverage across the three phrases: 0.9231, 0.8571, 0.8667.

Use the idea

Check train-test vocabulary coverage before choosing an encoder, and fit vocabularies within each training fold. Keep a representation only if held-out task performance improves, not just coverage.

Where the conclusion applies

Three training and three test phrases, lowercase letters and spaces only. Coverage measures what vocabulary is available, not predictive quality, and no threshold here says when embeddings are needed.

Check your understanding: At width 1 every phrase has coverage 1.0. Does that make single characters the better feature?
No. With 19 distinct training characters, every test character is known, so coverage reaches 1.0 without telling the words apart. Coverage only shows the vocabulary is available, not that the feature predicts anything.
