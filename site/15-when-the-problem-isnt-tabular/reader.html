When the Problem Isn't Tabular

Return a legal goal-reaching prefix-flip path.

A non-tabular task changes the input, the output contract and the budget, so check the legal output before trusting a score. One demonstration searches a three-pancake stack within a depth limit and replays the returned flips.

A legal goal-reaching prefix-flip path

Within a depth limit, can a bounded search return a legal flip path that actually reaches the sorted order, and what does failure mean?

A search must return a legal, completed solution, not just a promising estimate. Here the state is the pancake order, a move reverses the first k items, and the reported cost counts the flips in a path that is replayed and checked against the sorted order.

At depth 3 the search returns the flips 3 then 2 (2 flips), and the replay ends at the goal 123. It examined 5 states, so 6 - 5 = 1 of the 6 possible orders was never examined.

Worked steps

  1. Start 312, goal 123, depth limit 3.
  2. Path found: flips 3 then 2 (2 flips).
  3. States examined: 5 of 6 possible orders.
  4. Replay of the path ends at the goal.

Use the idea

Represent state and legal actions explicitly, return a success flag, retain the path and replay every returned solution. Keep a feasible completion cost separate from any proven lower bound.

Where the conclusion applies

A three-item instance searched exactly by breadth first search up to the depth limit. It illustrates goal checking and legal-path accounting, and is not a benchmark for beam search or NMCS or a reproduced Santa 2024 solution.

Check your understanding: Replaying the returned flips 3 then 2 on the stack 3, 1, 2, what is the order after the first flip?
Flipping the first 3 items of 3, 1, 2 gives 2, 1, 3. Flipping the first 2 items of that gives 1, 2, 3, the goal.
