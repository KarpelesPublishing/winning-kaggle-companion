Chapter 48

Pseudo-Labeling for CV

Trade coverage for a different population, not for correctness.

Build pseudo-labels fold by fold, log what was accepted, and judge the student on untouched real labels. One demonstration filters ten constructed predictions by confidence and counts what survives.

Every example is a constructed teaching example. Its results describe the declared inputs of the chapter activity, not a measured competition gain.

1. Coverage lost, errors kept: confidence filtering

Coverage lost, errors kept: confidence filtering

Can stricter pseudo-label confidence reduce coverage without removing systematic errors?

Ten constructed predictions are filtered by their top-class confidence, and the hidden labels are used only to count errors. Raising the threshold removes examples but selects a different group, not a guaranteed correct one. One very confident prediction is wrong, so it survives strict settings.

At confidence 0.85 the filter accepts 6 of 10 examples: 6 / 10 = 0.60. Of those, 2 are wrong, so 2 / 6 = 0.3333. Predicted class counts are 5 and 1, against 6 and 4 in the whole pool, so the accepted set drifts from the pool's balance.

Worked steps

  1. Examples with confidence at or above 0.85: 6 of 10.
  2. Acceptance fraction: 6 / 10 = 0.60.
  3. Accepted predicted classes: 5 in class 0 and 1 in class 1.
  4. Wrong labels among the accepted: 2.
  5. Error fraction: 2 / 6 = 0.3333.

Use the idea

Log acceptance, class balance and teacher provenance for every pseudo-labeling round. Judge the student on untouched real labels, never on the pseudo-labels themselves.

Where the conclusion applies

Ten examples with hand-set confidences, one of them confidently wrong. Hidden labels appear only for this audit and are never available when selecting real pseudo-labels. The counts are too small to estimate a general error rate.

Check your understanding: At threshold 0.95, which classes remain, and is the filter safe?
Only class 0 remains (3 accepted, 0 in class 1), and 1 of the 3 is wrong: the confident 0.99 prediction of class 0 whose true class is 1. The filter dropped class 1 entirely and kept the confident error.
