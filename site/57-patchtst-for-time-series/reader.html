PatchTST for Time Series and HAR

Count tokens from the stride, and check the tail.

Temporal patching turns short blocks of a signal into tokens, and overlap changes how many there are. One demonstration compares patch lengths on a 48-point signal.

1. Overlapping patches, token count and the uncovered tail

Demonstration 1 of 1

Overlapping patches, token count and the uncovered tail

How do patch length and an overlapping stride change the number of tokens, the coverage and the attention-pair count?

Overlapping patches advance by the stride, not by the patch length, so a signal gives floor((L - P) / S) + 1 tokens rather than L / P. When the division is uneven the last patch stops early and the tail is never covered by any patch. Attention cost for the token term scales with the square of the count.

Scroll sideways for the whole equation →

L is the signal length (48 points), P the patch length, S the stride (half of P), n the number of patches (tokens), and pairs the squared token count, a per-channel attention term.

Predict first. A 48-point signal is cut into patches of length 8 with stride 4. How many patches result?

With P = 8 and stride 4: (48 - 8) / 4 = 10, rounded down and plus 1 gives 11 patches, not 48 / 8 = 6. Attention pairs fall from 48 x 48 = 2304 to 11 x 11 = 121. The last patch ends at the final point, so nothing is left uncovered.

Worked steps

  1. Patch length 8, stride 4 = 8 / 2.
  2. Patch count: (48 - 8) / 4 = 10, rounded down plus 1 = 11.
  3. Attention pairs: 11 x 11 = 121, against 48 x 48 = 2304 for single-step tokens.
  4. Last patch ends at point 48, so 48 - 48 = 0 points are uncovered.
  5. Every covered point sits in at most 2 patches.

Use the idea

Compute the token count from the stride you actually use and check that the last patch reaches the end of the window. Profile the projection and prediction head too, because they are separate costs from attention.

Where the conclusion applies

A 48-point constructed signal, even patch lengths from 4 to 16, stride equal to half the patch length, and no padding. The pair count is one attention term per channel, not whole-model runtime, and no model is fitted.

Check your understanding: For patch length 10 and stride 5, how many points are left uncovered, and why?
Three. (48 - 10) / 5 = 7.6 rounds down to 7, so there are 8 patches and the last ends at point 45. Points 45, 46 and 47 are never covered.
