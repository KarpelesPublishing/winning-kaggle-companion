Chapter 57

PatchTST for Time Series and HAR

Compare patching with a feature baseline at matched forward origins, and choose patch length from the phenomena.

Patching turns short blocks into tokens, and the patch length decides what a token can see. One demonstration compares a patch-token stand-in with a feature baseline, the raw window and a seasonal-naive forecast on the same forward origins, and counts the tokens that each patch length creates.

A patch-token stand-in against a feature baseline, by patch length

Does patching beat a feature baseline at the same forward origins, and how does the patch length change the answer and the token count?

Short patches keep the local shape of a pulse, so the embedded tokens expose the oscillation that predicts the rebound. Long patches average that shape away inside one token, and with few tokens the model sees little more than a coarse summary, which the feature baseline also provides. Shorter patches cost more tokens, and attention cost grows with the square of the token count.

T is the window length (96), P the patch length, S the stride (P/2) and the number of tokens without padding is floor((T - P)/S) + 1. MAE is the mean absolute forecast error, so lower is better.

With patches of 12 steps (stride 6) each 96-step window becomes 15 tokens, floor((96 - 12) / 6) + 1 = 15. The patch model scores an MAE of 0.414 and beats the feature baseline at 0.447, so 0.447 - 0.414 = 0.033 separates them. The raw 96-step window scores 0.409 and the seasonal-naive forecast 0.653. Attention would relate 225 pairs of tokens per window.

Worked steps

  1. Tokens: floor((96 - 12) / 6) + 1 = 15.
  2. Feature baseline minus patch model: 0.447 - 0.414 = +0.033 (positive means patching is better).
  3. Raw window minus patch model: 0.409 - 0.414 = -0.005.
  4. Token pairs for attention: 15 x 15 = 225.

Use the idea

Compare a patch model with a feature baseline and the raw window on identical forward origins, pick the patch length from the duration of the events that matter, and check the token count and cost before a long run.

Where the conclusion applies

A stand-in, not PatchTST: patches share a PCA projection and feed gradient boosting, with no positional encoding, attention or end-to-end training, because the neural library is not installed here. The rebound makes the target depend on local pulse shape by construction, which favours a representation that sees shape; a signal that is a pure cycle gives the patch and feature models no such gap. One window length, one horizon and six sets; the raw-window comparator already uses all 96 values.

Check your understanding: A patch length of 4 scores best here. Why is that not simply the answer to use?
P = 4 creates 47 tokens against 15 for P = 12, about ten times as many token pairs for attention to relate, and its error gain is 0.025 (0.389 against 0.414). Whether that is worth the cost depends on the budget; the chapter asks for the actual token count and cost to be measured alongside the score. It also depends on the phenomena: the pulses here last about 10 steps.
