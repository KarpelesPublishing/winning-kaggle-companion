Advanced Target Encoding

Weight a category mean by how much its noise deserves.

Variance-aware shrinkage trusts a category mean less as its estimated noise grows. One demonstration moves the within-category variance and shows the encoded value slide from the sample mean toward the shared prior.

How category noise sets the pull toward the prior

How much should a noisy category mean be pulled toward the shared prior?

Two categories can share a sample mean yet deserve different trust. Here the constructed category has five observations with mean 0.8, a shared prior of 0.4 and a fixed between-category variance. Raising the within-category noise lowers the weight on the sample mean and moves the estimate toward the prior.

With within-variance 1.0, the weight on the category mean is 0.2 / (0.2 + 1.0 / 5) = 0.2 / 0.400 = 0.500. The estimate is 0.40 + 0.500 x 0.40 = 0.600, inside the range set by the prior 0.40 and the sample mean 0.80.

Worked steps

  1. Noise in the mean: 1.0 / 5 = 0.200.
  2. Weight on the category mean: 0.2 / (0.2 + 0.200) = 0.500.
  3. Weight on the prior: 1 - 0.500 = 0.500.
  4. Estimate: 0.40 + 0.500 x 0.40 = 0.600.

Use the idea

Keep the observation count separate from the estimated noise: count says how many rows you have, variance says how noisy each row is. Fit every prior and variance on training labels only, then compare the encoder against your smoothed-mean baseline under the same outer folds.

Where the conclusion applies

A normal-means-style illustration with a fixed prior mean, count and between-category variance; it is not a certified James-Stein estimator. Only the within-category variance changes here, and no hierarchy is modeled. Whether the encoder helps a model needs a nested validation comparison.

Check your understanding: At within-variance 2.0, what weight does the activity put on the category mean, and what is the estimate?
The weight is 0.2 / (0.2 + 2.0 / 5) = 0.2 / 0.6 = 0.333, so the estimate is 0.40 + 0.333 x 0.40 = 0.533.
