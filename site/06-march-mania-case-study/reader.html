Applying the offseason Elo boundary

How much of a rating gap should survive the season boundary, and what does the surviving gap say about the next game?

An Elo feature is a sequence of updates, and its meaning depends on which games and season boundaries enter it. At the boundary each rating is pulled toward 1500 by the carryover. Zero resets both teams, one keeps the whole gap, and values in between keep part of the prior information.

r is a team's rating before the offseason, r^+ its rating after, c the carryover between 0 and 1, d the retained rating gap between Team A and Team B, and P_A the Elo expected probability that Team A beats Team B.

Before the offseason the gap is 1512.0 - 1488.0 = 24.0. Carryover 0.65 keeps 0.65 x 24.0 = 15.6 points of it, so Team A enters the next game at 1507.8 against 1492.2, a win probability of 0.522.

Worked steps

  1. Ratings before the offseason: 1512.0 and 1488.0.
  2. Team A after: 1500 + 0.65 x (1512.0 - 1500) = 1507.8.
  3. Team B after: 1500 + 0.65 x (1488.0 - 1500) = 1492.2.
  4. Retained gap: 1507.8 - 1492.2 = 15.6.
  5. Team A win probability: 1 / (1 + 10^(-15.6 / 400)) = 0.522.

Use the idea

Regress once when the season changes, keep a trace of both teams' pregame ratings and updates, and build tournament snapshots only from games available by the submission cutoff. Compare carryover settings under chronological validation, not from one public result.

Where the conclusion applies

Two teams start equal at 1500, Team A wins one neutral game with K = 24 to reach 1512 and 1488, and no home advantage applies. This checks a season-boundary operation only: it identifies no best carryover for basketball and does not reproduce the chapter's ensemble.

Check your understanding: With carryover 1, what is Team A's win probability for the next game?
About 0.534. The full 24 point gap is kept, and 1 / (1 + 10^(-24 / 400)) = 0.534. At carryover 0 both teams are 1500 and the probability is exactly 0.5.
