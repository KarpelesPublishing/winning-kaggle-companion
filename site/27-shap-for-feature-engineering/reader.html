Adding contributions in log-odds, reading them in probability

Can you reconcile an additive explanation in log-odds units with the probability it produces?

An explanation can be additive without its numbers being additive probability changes. In this constructed binary model the contributions add exactly in log-odds, and the sigmoid converts the total to a probability. The same logit change moves the probability by different amounts depending on where you start.

z is the prediction logit, z_0 the baseline logit (-1.0), c_1 the other feature's contribution (0.3) and c_2 the controlled contribution (the control). p is the predicted probability, the sigmoid of z.

The logit adds exactly: (-1.00) + 0.30 + 0.80 = 0.10. The sigmoid turns 0.10 into probability 0.525, and 0.525 - 0.269 = 0.256 is the move from the baseline-only probability, which is not the sum of the logit pieces.

Worked steps

  1. Baseline logit -1.00, other feature 0.30, controlled feature 0.80.
  2. Sum in logit units: (-1.00) + 0.30 + 0.80 = 0.10.
  3. Sigmoid: 1 / (1 + e^(-z)) at z = 0.10 gives 0.525.
  4. Baseline-only probability: 0.269.
  5. Probability change: 0.525 - 0.269 = 0.256.

Use the idea

Name the output unit of your explanation and check that the expected value plus the contributions matches the model output in that unit on held-out rows. Turn one dependency pattern into one falsifiable ablation, not a claim about gain.

Where the conclusion applies

Two invented contributions added to a binary logit; they are not computed SHAP values. An attribution explains a fitted predictor under a stated configuration and does not establish a causal effect or the gain from a new feature. The check is the arithmetic of units only.

Check your understanding: With the controlled contribution at -2.0, what are the prediction logit and probability?
The logit is -1.0 + 0.3 + (-2.0) = -2.7, and the sigmoid gives p = 0.063, which is 0.206 below the baseline-only 0.269.
