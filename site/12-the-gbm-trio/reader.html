A Huber transition on five residuals

When you change the Huber delta, how do the loss and the gradient of large and small residuals change?

Huber loss is quadratic for small residuals and linear beyond delta, so a large residual pulls on the update with a capped strength while its sign still says which way to move. Delta moves the transition and with it the influence of outliers.

With delta 2, residual 8 is past the transition, so its loss is 2 x (8 - 1) = 14 and its gradient is capped at 2. 4 of 5 rows still have a nonzero gradient.

Worked steps

  1. Delta: 2. Residuals: -8, -2, 0, 2, 8.
  2. Losses: 14, 2, 0, 2, 14.
  3. Gradients (residual clipped to plus or minus delta): -2, -2, 0, 2, 2.
  4. Rows with nonzero gradient: 4 of 5.

Use the idea

Check residual scale, target units and the framework's exact loss definition before choosing a delta, then compare squared error and a robust loss on the same held-out rows with the competition metric.

Where the conclusion applies

Mathematical Huber loss on five fixed residuals, not a specific CatBoost or other framework implementation. A small delta does not by itself force a model to stop at zero iterations.
