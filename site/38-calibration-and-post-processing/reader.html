Chapter 38

Calibration and Post-Processing

Fit post-processing on development rows and assess it elsewhere.

Treat post-processing as another fitted model with its own development and assessment rows. One demonstration shows temperature scaling moving log loss while the ranking stays fixed.

1. Temperature scaling changes log loss, not ranking

Demonstration 1 of 1

Temperature scaling changes log loss, not ranking

Can temperature scaling improve log loss while leaving ranking unchanged?

Dividing logits by a positive temperature keeps their order but moves probabilities toward or away from one half. Two middle rows here have outcomes that disagree with the confidence pattern, so softer probabilities lower the penalty on them. A probability-sensitive score changes while the ranking does not.

Scroll sideways for the whole equation →

z_i is the logit of row i, y_i its outcome (1 or 0), n the number of rows (four here), T the temperature, and sigma(u) = 1 / (1 + e^(-u)) converts a scaled logit to a probability. The activity also reports the Brier score, the mean of (y_i - p_i)^2 with p_i = sigma(z_i/T).

At T = 1.5 the log loss is 0.5741 against 0.6657 with no scaling (T = 1.0): 0.6657 - 0.5741 = 0.0916, which is lower. The four probabilities 0.0650, 0.3392, 0.6608, 0.9350 still rise in the same order as the logits, so a ranking metric cannot tell the difference.

Worked steps

  1. Divide each logit by T = 1.5: -2.667, -0.667, 0.667, 2.667.
  2. Apply the sigmoid: probabilities 0.0650, 0.3392, 0.6608, 0.9350, still in increasing order.
  3. Per-row loss, minus the log of the probability given to the actual outcome: 0.0672, 1.0810, 1.0810, 0.0672. Their mean is the log loss, 0.5741.
  4. Brier score, the mean squared gap between outcome and probability: 0.2204.

Use the idea

Fit a monotone calibration such as positive temperature on development predictions, and judge it on separate rows with the competition metric. Report the ranking metric alongside, since it will not move.

Where the conclusion applies

Four constructed logits and outcomes, with positive temperature so order is preserved. Picking T from these four labels makes them tuning data, so the best score is a tuning result, not independent calibration evidence.

Check your understanding: Raising T from 1.0 to 1.5 cuts log loss from 0.6657 to 0.5741. Why does the highest-ranked row stay the same?
Dividing every logit by the same positive T is monotone, so the order of the rows cannot change. Only the distances from probability 0.5 shrink, which changes log loss and Brier score but not the ranking.
