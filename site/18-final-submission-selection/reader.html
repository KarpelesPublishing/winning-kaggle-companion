A backup within an explicit score tolerance

Among candidates whose CV is close enough to the primary's, which one does a lowest-correlation rule pick as the backup, and when does it pick none?

Final selection starts from the competition's rules and a registry of validated candidates. A backup can hedge a different failure mode, but only candidates close enough in CV to the primary are eligible, and among them the rule prefers the lowest correlation as a transparent heuristic.

Candidate 3 has CV 0.881, which is 0.885 - 0.881 = 0.004 below the primary, within the tolerance 0.005. Of the 2 eligible, it has the lowest correlation, 0.90. That marks it for inspection, not as a proven hedge.

Worked steps

  1. Primary CV: 0.885. Tolerance: 0.005.
  2. Eligible candidates (CV within the tolerance of the primary): 2.
  3. Lowest correlation among them: candidate 3 at 0.90, CV 0.881.
  4. Decision: inspect distinct error slices.

Use the idea

Check a proposed backup's error slices, calibration and pipeline before trusting that its difference is real diversity, and promote a clearly better challenger instead of demoting it for being correlated.

Where the conclusion applies

A primary and three candidates with fixed illustrative CV scores and correlations, higher score is better. The rule is a heuristic hedge, not an optimized probability of surviving the private leaderboard, and correlation alone does not measure useful diversity.
