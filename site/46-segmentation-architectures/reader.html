Segmentation Architectures

Know which Dice reduction the competition scores.

1. Pooled Dice versus mean per-image Dice

Demonstration 1 of 1

Pooled Dice versus mean per-image Dice

When do pooled Dice and mean per-image Dice disagree after probability-map thresholding?

Two small probability maps are thresholded against two true masks, one large and one tiny. Dice is computed per image, averaged, and also pooled from summed overlaps and mask sizes. The large mask dominates the pooled score, while both images count equally in the mean.

P_i is the predicted mask for image i (probability at or above the threshold), Y_i its true mask, n the number of images (two here), and |.| a pixel count. An image with both masks empty scores Dice 1 by the activity's convention.

Mean per-image Dice adds the two image scores and halves: 0.8571 + 0.0000 = 0.8571, then 0.8571 / 2 = 0.4286. Pooled Dice sums the overlaps first: 6 / 8 = 0.7500. The two reductions differ by 0.7500 - 0.4286 = 0.3214 because the tiny-foreground image is one of two images in the mean but supplies only 1 of the 8 mask pixels in the pooled denominator.

Worked steps

  1. Image 0: 4 true and 3 predicted pixels, overlap 3, Dice 0.8571.
  2. Image 1: 1 true and 0 predicted pixels, overlap 0, Dice 0.0000.
  3. Mean per image: 0.8571 / 2 = 0.4286.
  4. Pooled: 2 x 3 = 6, and 8 pixels in total, so 6 / 8 = 0.7500.

Use the idea

Read the official scorer to learn whether it averages by image, class, instance or pooled pixels, and what it does with empty masks. Implement that exact reduction beside your training loss.

Where the conclusion applies

Two images and six pixels each, with fixed probabilities and masks: no network is trained. Empty-empty masks score 1 by convention. The numbers show how reductions differ, not which one your competition uses.

Check your understanding: At threshold 0.9, what are the mean and pooled Dice, and why is pooled higher?
Image 0 predicts one pixel (Dice 0.4) and image 1 predicts none (Dice 0), so the mean is (0.4 + 0) / 2 = 0.2. Pooled is 2 x 1 / 6 = 0.3333, higher because image 1 adds only 1 of 6 pixels.
