The Fourth-Root Blend

Compare margin weighting exponents.

Start from equal averaging and treat margin weights as a heuristic to be validated. One demonstration compares the weights that three illustrative scores receive at different exponents.

Margin weights at different exponents

When blend weights come from score margins raised to a power, how far do they move from equal weighting as the exponent changes?

A blend's weights can be built from each model's improvement over a baseline. A fractional exponent compresses the differences between margins, which is a heuristic for avoiding extreme weights and not a covariance-aware optimum.

Scroll sideways for the whole equation →

s_i is the score of model i, b the baseline score, s_i - b its margin, p the exponent (0.25 is the fourth root) and w_i its normalized blend weight.

Margins are 0.742 - 0.50 = 0.242 for model 1, down to 0.229 for model 3. With exponent 0.25 the weights are 0.3353 and 0.3307, a ratio of 0.3353 / 0.3307 = 1.014, close to equal weighting.

Worked steps

  1. Margins: 0.742 - 0.50 = 0.242, then 0.238 and 0.229.
  2. Raise each margin to the power 0.25, then divide by the sum of the three.
  3. Weights: 0.3353, 0.3339, 0.3307 (sum 1.0000).
  4. Best to worst weight ratio: 0.3353 / 0.3307 = 1.014.

Use the idea

Define the baseline and the score direction before forming margins, then compare equal, proportional and compressed weights on held-out predictions and confirm the chosen blend on data not used to pick the weights.

Where the conclusion applies

Three illustrative micro-F1 scores, higher is better, with the activity's assumed majority baseline of 0.50 (a stated value, not one measured from a dummy predictor). The chapter's own code example uses a reference of 1/3 instead, which gives fourth-root weights of about 0.3345, 0.3337 and 0.3318, so its numbers differ from the ones here. No claim is made that these weights are optimal or that the blend beats its best member.

Check your understanding: At exponent 1, what weight does model 1 get, and how do you get it by hand?
The margins sum to 0.242 + 0.238 + 0.229 = 0.709, so model 1 gets 0.242 / 0.709 = 0.3413.
