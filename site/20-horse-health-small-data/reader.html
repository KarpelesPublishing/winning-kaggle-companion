Horse Health: Small Data, Big Discipline

Check what micro-F1 can and cannot tell you on a small dataset.

Small-data discipline starts with knowing what the official metric measures and where adjustments are assessed. One demonstration shows that micro-F1 equals accuracy for single-label rows at every class multiplier.

1. Micro-F1 and accuracy on single-label rows

Demonstration 1 of 1

Micro-F1 and accuracy on single-label rows

When you rescale one class score and take the argmax, can micro-F1 ever move away from accuracy?

For exhaustive single-label multiclass prediction, every correct row is one true positive and every wrong row is one false positive plus one false negative. Substituting those counts turns micro-F1 into the fraction of correct rows. Rescaling a class score can change which rows are right, but the two metrics move together.

Scroll sideways for the whole equation →

F1_micro is F1 computed from counts pooled over all classes. TP, FP and FN are true positives, false positives and false negatives pooled over all classes; C is the number of correct rows and N the number of rows. With one label per row, FP = FN = N - C.

Predict first. At multiplier 1 the activity gets 4 of 5 rows right (0.80). Raise the class 2 multiplier to 2. What happens?

At multiplier 1.0, 4 of 5 rows are right, so accuracy is 4 / 5 = 0.80. Each wrong row adds one false positive and one false negative, so FP = FN = 1: micro-F1 = 2 x 4 / (2 x 4 + 1 + 1) = 8 / 10 = 0.80. The multiplier changes which rows are right; it never separates the two metrics.

Worked steps

  1. Class 2 multiplier 1.0 gives predicted labels 0, 1, 2, 0, 1.
  2. Correct rows: 4 of 5. Wrong rows: 5 - 4 = 1.
  3. Accuracy: 4 / 5 = 0.80.
  4. Micro-F1: 2 x 4 / (2 x 4 + 1 + 1) = 0.80.

Use the idea

Check that the target is single-label and every class is included before treating the two metrics as the same. If you tune class multipliers, fit them inside the selection boundary and assess on outer predictions or an untouched local holdout.

Where the conclusion applies

Five constructed rows and three classes, with no fitted model. The identity needs single-label rows and all classes in the average; it does not hold for multilabel data. Equal metrics say nothing about whether a multiplier helps on new rows, and a gain measured on the labels used to tune it is in-sample.

Check your understanding: At multiplier 0.5 the activity gets 3 of 5 rows right. What is micro-F1, computed from TP, FP and FN?
TP = 3 and FP = FN = 5 - 3 = 2, so micro-F1 = 2 x 3 / (2 x 3 + 2 + 2) = 6 / 10 = 0.60, equal to accuracy 3 / 5.
