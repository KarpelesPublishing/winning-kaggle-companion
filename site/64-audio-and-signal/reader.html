Audio and Signal Competitions

Make training audio look like the test set, and measure it.

Match training clips to the intended test recordings and check what augmentation actually did. One demonstration mixes noise at a requested decibel level and measures the result.

1. Mix noise at a requested signal-to-noise ratio

Demonstration 1 of 1

Mix noise at a requested signal-to-noise ratio

Does the requested signal-to-noise ratio match the power of the noise that is actually mixed into the signal?

Background mixing is one way to make training audio look like the test recordings. The noise is rescaled so its power, relative to the signal, gives the requested decibel level, then added. Measuring the achieved ratio before training confirms the mixing did what was asked.

P_s is the signal power and P_n the raw noise power (mean squared amplitude), SNR the requested ratio in decibels, a the factor the noise is multiplied by, and a^2 P_n the added noise power.

10 dB is a power ratio of 10^(10/10) = 10.000, so the added noise power is 0.500 / 10.000 = 0.0500. The two waveforms are orthogonal over the 64 samples, so the powers add: 0.500 + 0.0500 = 0.5500. This checks the mixing arithmetic only; it does not show the noise matches the test recordings.

Worked steps

  1. Signal power 0.500. A requested 10 dB is a power ratio of 10^(10/10) = 10.000.
  2. Target noise power: 0.500 / 10.000 = 0.0500.
  3. Raw noise power is 0.500, so the scale is the square root of (0.0500 / 0.500) = 0.3162.
  4. Measured SNR: 10 x log10(0.500 / 0.0500) = 10.00 dB.
  5. Mixed power: 0.500 + 0.0500 = 0.5500.

Use the idea

Match noise lengths to the signal, then measure the actual mixed power before any clipping or normalization. Compare mixing against other augmentations on held-out recordings, since a constructed ratio does not make the noise representative.

Where the conclusion applies

Two equal-length constructed waveforms of nonzero power, with no clipping or amplitude normalization after mixing. Power is the mean squared amplitude, and the signal and noise do not overlap in frequency, so their powers add. No audio file or spectrogram model is involved.

Check your understanding: At 0 dB, what is the noise scale, and what is the mixed power?
The noise scale is 1.0, because the added noise power equals the signal power of 0.5, so the mixed power is 0.5 + 0.5 = 1.0.
