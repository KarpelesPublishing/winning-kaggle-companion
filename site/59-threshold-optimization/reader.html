Chapter 59

Threshold Optimization

Fit a cutoff on one partition and judge it on another.

A decision rule should match the score, and a fitted threshold has a distribution boundary. One demonstration tunes a cutoff on eight rows and checks it on a separate audit set.

Every example is a constructed teaching example. Its results describe the declared inputs of the chapter activity, not a measured competition gain.

A tuned cutoff on held-out audit rows

Does the cutoff that maximizes F1 on the tuning rows still win on a separate audit set?

Two constructed eight-row tables hold a probability and a label per row. The cutoff is searched on the tuning rows only, then frozen and scored on the audit rows. Move the cutoff to see tuning and audit F1 disagree about which value is best.

At cutoff 0.50 the tuning rows give TP 3, FP 1, FN 1, so F1 = 2 x 3 / (6 + 2) = 0.750. The tuning-best cutoff 0.40 scores 0.889 on tuning but 0.500 on audit, a drop of 0.889 - 0.500 = 0.389: the tuning score overstates what the frozen cutoff does on new rows.

Worked steps

  1. Selected cutoff 0.50: tuning TP 3, FP 1, FN 1.
  2. Tuning F1 = 2 x 3 / (6 + 2) = 0.750.
  3. Audit counts at the same cutoff: TP 2, FP 2, FN 1, F1 0.571.
  4. Cutoff 0.40 is best on tuning (0.889) and scores 0.500 on audit.
  5. Tuning-to-audit drop at the frozen cutoff: 0.889 - 0.500 = 0.389.

Use the idea

Fit post-processing such as a cutoff on one partition and keep an untouched audit partition for the frozen choice. Log the confusion counts, and report the tuning gain separately from the audit result.

Where the conclusion applies

Two illustrative eight-row subsets with different error patterns, positive-class F1 only (not macro F1 or MAP). With 8 rows per subset, one row moves F1 a lot, so the audit result is a demonstration, not a statistical estimate. Good ranking is separate from good calibration.

Check your understanding: At cutoff 0.5, how many audit rows are false positives and false negatives, and what is the audit F1?
Two false positives and one false negative with two true positives: F1 = 2 x 2 / (4 + 3) = 0.571.
